Stefano Morello is a multi-modal author, scholar, educator, and translator, currently serving as Assistant Director for Digital Projects at the American Social History Project/Center for Media and Learning, a research center of the City University of New York.

His scholarly work combines digital and traditional methods to investigate the infrastructure and the transnational reverberations of American popular and unpopular culture.

Stefano holds a PhD in English with a certificate in American Studies from the CUNY Graduate Center. He has been honored with fellowships and awards from the New York Public Library, the Wellcome Hub, the Italian Ministry for Education, University and Research, the Canadian Social Knowledge Institute, and several research centers across the CUNY system.

He's also a founding editor of JAm It! (Journal of American Studies in Italy), a member of the Journal of Interactive Technology and Pedagogy editorial collective, and the editor of the Italian American Studies Open Syllabus.

Projects

Italian American Studies Open Syllabus

A crowd-sourced digital collection of remixable resources to explore and teach the Italian diaspora in the United States.

Link

East Bay Punk Digital Archive

A digital repository to preserve and make accessible zines, artworks, and other ephemera produced by participants in the punk-rock scene that loomed in and around the San Francisco Bay Area (1980s—1990s)

Link

The Lung Block

The Lung Block: A New York City Slum & Its Forgotten Italian Immigrant Community is a physical and digital exhibition co-curated with Kerri Culhane that constrasts the Progressive Era narrative of NYC's infamous Lung Block as an epicenter of disease with the lived experience of its local residents.

Link

Objects, Aesthetics, and Narratives of Italy in the United States 1949–1972

A bi-lingual digital exhibition and OER that leverages design, visual art, literature, film, and commercial goods to tell the story of the transatlantic transfers and cultural exchanges between Italy and the United States in the three decades following the end of WWII. Co-curated with Marta Averna, Gioele Cristofari, Stefano Morello, Matteo Sant'Andrea, and Marta Zonca.

The Beats in/and Italy

A storymap that builds upon the final projects of the students in the first-ever course at the nexus of US literary studies and DH ever offered in Italy, co-taught with Cristina luli at the University of Eastern Piedmont.

Link

Let My People Know

Let My People Know: The Story of Wilfred Mendelson, ‘Mendy,’ August 17, 1915 - July 28, 1938 is a digital edition critically annotated by the students in the Spring 2022 course “Activism and the College Experience,” co-taught with Isabel Estrada at The City College of New York.

Link

CUNY Distance Learning Archive

A crowdsourced archive of personal and institutional documentation related to CUNY’s move to online learning in the wake of the COVID-19 crisis. Co-curated with Travis M. Bartley, Nicole Cote, Matthew K. Gold, and Zach Muhlbauer.

Link

Ellis Island

Recovery and redesign of Robert Viscusi's sonnet generator—a project inspired by Raymond Queneau and the Oulipo tradition that uses an algorithm to generate over a duodecillion unique sonnets, echoing immigrants’ fragmentary paths of self-reinvention in a new world.

Link
